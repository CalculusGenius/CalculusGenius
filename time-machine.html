Laboratory

A place to experiment with calculus rather than merely read it. Investigate functions, limits, derivatives, integrals and approximations; alter parameters; observe structure; and keep a compact record of what you discover.

Try x^3 - 4*x, sin(x)/x, exp(-x^2), or log(abs(x)). The graph is sampled numerically; derivatives are estimated locally.

Increase n and compare the numerical approximation with the limiting integral. The visual partition is deliberately exposed.

Observe Newton's method as an actual sequence x₀, x₁, … rather than only as a formula. A derivative is computed numerically.

The coefficients are estimated from finite differences. Use the experiment to investigate where a Taylor polynomial approximates the original function well.

Experimental discipline

Observe → conjecture → verify

Change one parameter at a time. Look for invariants, singular behaviour, convergence, symmetry and failure cases. Then ask what hypotheses would be required to turn the observation into a theorem.

A useful experiment is not one that merely produces a beautiful graph. It is one that makes you ask a sharper mathematical question.
